# Generating Leads From the Website — What We Need From You

**Date:** 7 August 2026 · **Site live since:** 4 August 2026
**Covers:** the first 30 days of lead generation (6 Aug – 5 Sep)

The website is live and everything on it works. We have now tested the full enquiry path end to
end — form submitted, enquiry recorded, confirmation email delivered to the sender, notification
sent to `sales@`. **The machine is working.**

What we do not yet have is leads. This note explains why, and asks for the handful of things only
you can give us. Each item is numbered so you can reply by reference — you do not need to answer
all of them at once, but **items 1 and 2 hold up most of the work.**

---

## Where we actually are

In the 30 days to 7 August:

| | |
|---|---|
| People who visited the site | **129** (647 pages viewed) |
| Of those, reached the Contact page | **26** |
| Started filling in an enquiry form | **0** |
| Enquiries received | **0** |
| Job applications received | **1** |

Where those visitors came from: 104 direct (typed the address or followed a link with no source),
10 Google, 5 ChatGPT, 2 Bing, 2 LinkedIn, 1 Facebook, and a handful of others.

**Three things this tells us:**

1. **Most visitors never get as far as the Contact page.** 103 of 129 left before reaching it. The
   problem sits earlier in the journey than the enquiry form.
2. **We are invisible in search for what you actually sell.** Over three months, searches
   containing "outsourcing" brought the site **zero** visits. Not few — zero.
3. **Almost everyone who arrives already knows the name.** There is no channel bringing in people
   who have not heard of you. The website is currently a brochure for existing contacts, not a
   source of new ones.

There is also a specific problem worth knowing about: **most of the site's search impressions are
people looking for *PPL Electric Utilities*, a large American energy company.** Those visitors will
never become customers, and they distort the numbers. We can work around it, but it means the
generic term "PPL" is not winnable and we should not try.

## What we need from you

### 1. Who is your ideal customer? *(Decision — blocks the most)*

Not "any business that needs outsourcing". Specifically:

- **What industries** do your best clients come from?
- **What size** are they — headcount, or number of seats they typically contract?
- **Where** are they based — US, Australia, UK, local?
- **Who signs the contract** — a founder, a COO, a head of customer service?

**Why this is first:** everything else depends on it. We cannot write pages that rank, or choose
what to advertise, without knowing who we are writing for. Right now the site describes what .ppl
does; it does not speak to a particular buyer.

### 2. What would that customer type into Google? *(Content)*

In your own words — the services as a client would describe them, not as the industry does.
Ten or fifteen phrases is plenty. For example: *"outsourced customer support Philippines"*,
*"offshore accounting team"*, *"BPO for e-commerce"*.

**Why it matters:** this is the list we build content against. **Without it, the search work
cannot start** — we would be guessing at the words your buyers use, and the current zero
visibility suggests our guesses so far have not been right.

### 3. Proof we can publish *(Content — biggest credibility gap)*

Buyers in this market shortlist on evidence, and the site currently offers none. We are asking for:

- **Case studies**, even fully anonymised — *"a US healthcare provider, 40 seats, 30% cost
  reduction in year one"* is enough. No client names needed if that is a problem.
- **Testimonials**, with permission to attribute.
- **Any numbers you are willing to stand behind** — seats delivered, client retention, average
  time to fill a team.

**Why it matters:** this is the single biggest gap between your site and your competitors'. It is
also the thing we genuinely cannot write for you — we will not invent results on your behalf.

### 4. Do you have a Google Business Profile? *(Access)*

If yes, we need access. If no, we should create one.

**Why it matters:** it is free, quick, and it is the best single fix for the *PPL Electric*
confusion — it tells Google you are a distinct company in Pasig. It also makes you findable in
local and map searches.

### 5. Is there a budget for paid advertising? *(Decision — yes or no is enough for now)*

We are not proposing a campaign yet. We need to know whether one is on the table, because it
changes what we do in weeks three and four.

**Our honest view:** organic search will not produce results within a month — that work compounds
over quarters. A small, tightly targeted paid test would buy us *data* about which messages land,
much faster. But it only makes sense once items 1–3 are answered, or we would be paying to send
strangers to a page with no proof on it.

### 6. Who writes for the site, and how often? *(Decision)*

The blog is built, and staff can publish without us. Six posts are live. But there is currently no
schedule and no owner.

**Why it matters:** content is the only realistic route to being found by people who do not already
know you. One useful article a fortnight, aimed at a real buying question, compounds. Nothing
published means nothing changes.

*Related, and worth a decision:* every staff login can currently edit and publish anything. If you
would prefer some people to draft while only certain people publish, tell us and we will build
that. If mutual trust is fine, we will leave it as is.

### 7. What happens to an enquiry when it arrives? *(Decision)*

Enquiries land in `sales@` and in the website admin panel, where they can be marked
*new → contacted → qualified → won / lost*.

**We have also set up a HubSpot CRM account for you, on the free plan, at no cost.** The sales
pipeline is built and matches the 3Ds language you already use:

> New Lead → Consultation Booked → Discover → Design → Deliver → Won / Lost

**To be clear about the money:** we are not recommending you *pay* for a CRM. At the current volume
a paid subscription would be an empty database that gets abandoned. We set up the free plan because
it costs nothing and means the pipeline already exists when volume arrives, rather than being a
project you have to start later. A paid plan is worth revisiting once you are consistently seeing
ten to fifteen enquiries a month.

⚠ **One thing to action:** the account currently has a **14-day free trial of a paid tier**
attached. Everything we built works on the free plan, so **nothing needs to be bought and the trial
can simply be allowed to lapse.** Please don't upgrade on our account — if HubSpot prompts you, you
can ignore it.

What we still need from you:

- **Who owns follow-up**, and how quickly? The site currently promises a reply within 1–2 business
  days on your behalf.
- **What counts as "qualified"** to you? Our definitions are currently a guess, and they are what
  every later conversion number will be measured against.
- **Do the pipeline stages above match how you actually sell?** They are our reading of your 3Ds
  framework. If a real deal moves differently, tell us and we will change them — better now, while
  the pipeline is empty.

**This question also unlocks a separate one.** We have asked, in the access note sent alongside
this, for permission to connect the sales team's Microsoft 365 mailboxes to HubSpot, so that replies
to an enquiry are logged against that customer rather than living in one person's inbox. That only
makes sense once we know **whose** mailbox — so answering "who owns follow-up" answers both.

### 8. Two time-sensitive housekeeping items *(Decision — needed within 10 days)*

- **Three job postings expire on 17 August** and one on 24 August. After that the Careers page is
  empty. Careers is one of your most-visited pages, so it is a real entry point to lose. Please
  confirm which roles should be renewed, closed, or replaced.
- **Two blog posts are saved as drafts with future dates** (14 and 28 August). Dates alone do not
  publish them — someone needs to click Publish on the day. If you would like them to publish
  automatically on a set date, we can build that; just say so.

## In short

If you can only answer three things, make them:

1. **Who your ideal customer is** (item 1)
2. **The words they would search for** (item 2)
3. **One or two case studies we can publish** (item 3)

Those three unblock everything else. Items 4–7 shape the second half of the month, and item 8 needs
an answer within ten days regardless.

**One honest caveat on timing:** search rankings realistically move over months two to four, not
weeks. What this first month can deliver is a working funnel, the leak closed, the content engine
started, and the first enquiries out of the traffic you already have. Judge it on that.
